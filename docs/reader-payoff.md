# Reader payoff: what the last "so" has to buy

[`causal-chain.md`](causal-chain.md) says the owner line is the chain's last "so": this is what we measured, so this is what changes for you. It does not say what "changes for you" has to be. A chain can hold at every joint and still end on something nobody would pay for ("so the header is now set correctly"). The owner added the missing half on 2026-11-14: people pay for three things, so the last "so" names one of them.

**Scope.** The same copy the chain rule covers: posts, pages, package and industry copy, emails, teaser cards, meta descriptions. Code comments and instruction text stay as they are.

## The three payoffs

| Payoff          | What the reader is paying for                                                                       | Examples                                                                                      | The rule it follows                                                                                  |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Solution**    | A real, pressing problem or discomfort made to disappear: pain, debt, stress, confusion, loneliness | A plumber for a midnight pipe leak, a doctor for an illness, an accountant to fix tax trouble | The bigger and more urgent the pain, the less people hesitate or argue over the price                |
| **Convenience** | Skipping the hard way: time saved, friction and effort removed                                      | Food delivery, one-click checkout, a car wash instead of scrubbing by hand                    | The richest businesses often win simply by making things faster, simpler and less of a headache      |
| **Experience**  | A specific emotion, status, a memory, a story to tell                                               | A concert, a theme park, a specialty coffee bought for the feeling as much as the caffeine    | The reader is not buying the item or the service; they are buying the emotional state attached to it |

## The rule

**The last "so" of a piece, and the owner line that closes each gain section, names one payoff in the reader's own terms.** A problem that goes away, a step they no longer take, or a feeling they get to have. A change to the system ("so the cache is warm", "so SPF now passes") is a link in the middle of the chain; it needs one more "so" before the chain is finished ("so the invoice email stops going to spam").

How each payoff shapes the chain that leads to it:

- **Solution.** The first cause is the pain, in literal terms: what broke, when, what it cost. Urgency is stated with the fact that makes it urgent (the outage hour, the bounced invoice, the fine), never with adjectives. The last "so" is the pain gone. Because urgent pain argues for itself, this chain needs the least persuasion and the most precision.
- **Convenience.** The first cause is the hard way, counted: the steps, the minutes, the tabs, the retyped command. The last "so" is the same job with the count lower. The practitioner's version of this payoff is the command and the habit; the owner's version is the hours or the hassle that left.
- **Experience.** The first cause is the moment, in sensory terms. The last "so" is the state the reader ends up in: calm on a Monday, proud of the site, sure enough to stop checking. Name the state plainly, once. Claiming a feeling the facts above it did not earn is the adjective problem `voice.md` already bans.

One primary payoff per piece. A second can appear as a "but" or a later section's close ("it fixes the bounce, but the part people mention is never thinking about DNS again"); three at once is a brochure, and a brochure is a list.

## By block

| Block                           | How the rule applies                                                                         |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Owner line                      | Names the payoff. This is the main place the rule is checked.                                |
| TL;DR                           | The last bullet is the payoff, or what to do to get it.                                      |
| Meta description                | The second sentence is the payoff or the reversal that leads to it.                          |
| Emails                          | The first line after the subject says which payoff the reader gets; the ask follows from it. |
| Teaser card lines               | The hook is the pain, the hard way or the moment; the payoff line is its consequence.        |
| Package and industry copy       | Open on the pain or the hard way the package removes; price comes after the payoff is named. |
| Tables, captions, Key Takeaways | Exempt, as they are from the chain.                                                          |

## The payoff test

Run it after the three chain tests, on the last block of the piece and on each owner line:

1. **Which of the three?** Say it in one word. If the answer is "none, it describes the system", the chain stops one link short.
2. **Whose terms?** The sentence should survive being read to someone who does not know what the tool is. "So the report runs in 4 seconds" passes for a practitioner; the owner line needs "so the Monday numbers are there before the coffee is".
3. **Earned?** Trace the payoff back up the chain. Every link down to it must already be on the page. If the payoff needs a fact the piece does not carry, that is a new fact, not a joint.

## In a rechain

A rechain may not add facts, and that limit holds here. When an owner line fails the payoff test:

- If the payoff is already in the article (often mid-section, or in the FAQ), move it or rewrite the closing sentence so the chain ends on it. That is a joint edit.
- If the article carries no payoff at all, do not invent one. Report it ("section X ends on a system change; no payoff in the copy") and leave the choice to the owner: supply the fact, or send the post to a rewrite that is allowed new facts.

This test is a reading, like the swap test, so `rechain-check.php` does not enforce it. Say in the report which payoff the piece ends on.
